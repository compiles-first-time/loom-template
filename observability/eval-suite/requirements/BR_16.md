# BR_16 — Claim provenance is resolved by code, and caps confidence

> Register per [ADR-0046](../../../adr/0046-requirements-exceptions-testcase-registry.md),
> schema per [ADR-0061](../../../adr/0061-requirements-register-role-and-verifier-fields.md).
> Implements [ADR-0060](../../../adr/0060-claim-provenance-verification.md).
> Tests: [`scripts/lib/claim-provenance.test.mjs`](../../../scripts/lib/claim-provenance.test.mjs) — 66/66.

The ask: the claim convention required `sources` and **validated none of them** — a hand-typed array was byte-identical to a verified one, so Rule 22's provenance requirement was satisfiable by typing. Deployed models hallucinate 11–57% of citations, and Dahl et al. (*Large Legal Fictions*, J. Legal Analysis, >800k questions) find models **cannot predict their own hallucinations** — which forecloses self-assessment and forces a deterministic resolver.

## Known limits

- **V2 is unreachable in this environment.** With arxiv.org, doi.org, huggingface.co and metr.org all proxy-blocked, every external citation caps at `V1` (0.80) and cannot support a `>95%` autonomous-execution claim. That is the correct behaviour, not a bug — but it means the evidence review's claims are all capped until run from an unrestricted network.
- **`V4` (replicated primary) is defined but never assigned automatically.** Nothing mechanically distinguishes a replication from a citation; it is reserved for manual promotion.
